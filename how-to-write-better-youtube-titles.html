How to Write Better YouTube Titles

Your YouTube title is one of the first things viewers see when they discover your video. A strong title should explain the topic clearly, create curiosity and accurately represent the content. This guide shows you how to create better YouTube titles without relying on misleading clickbait.

1. Start With the Main Topic

Before writing your title, identify the primary subject of the video. Think about the question your audience is asking or the problem your video solves.

For example, if your video teaches beginners how to edit videos, your main topic might be "video editing for beginners." Use that idea as the foundation of your title.

2. Put Important Words Near the Beginning

YouTube titles have limited space, especially on mobile devices. Put the most important words near the beginning whenever possible.

Instead of: "Everything You Need to Know Before You Start Learning Video Editing"

Consider: "Video Editing for Beginners: Complete Starter Guide"

3. Make the Benefit Clear

A viewer should quickly understand why they should watch your video. Explain the benefit, result or information they will receive.

  • How to edit videos faster
  • 5 mistakes beginners should avoid
  • How to create better thumbnails
  • A beginner's guide to YouTube SEO

4. Use Curiosity Carefully

Curiosity can make a title more interesting, but it should not hide what the video is actually about. Misleading titles may attract clicks but can disappoint viewers.

A good approach is to combine a clear topic with an interesting angle.

5. Use Natural Keywords

Include words your target audience might actually search for, but avoid stuffing your title with unnecessary keywords.

For example, a title such as "YouTube SEO for Beginners: 7 Simple Ways to Improve Your Videos" communicates both the topic and the value of the video naturally.

6. Avoid Misleading Clickbait

Strong titles do not need to make promises your video cannot deliver. Make sure the title matches the actual content.

Quick tip: Write three to five possible titles before choosing the final version. Compare them for clarity, relevance, curiosity and accuracy.

Final Checklist

  • Does the title clearly describe the video?
  • Is the main topic easy to understand?
  • Does it communicate a useful benefit?
  • Are important keywords used naturally?
  • Does the title accurately represent the video?
  • Would a viewer understand the topic quickly on mobile?
